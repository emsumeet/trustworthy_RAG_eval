# Trustworthy RAG Evaluation

A research-oriented project investigating how **retrieval quality** and **evidence sufficiency** affect the reliability of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) systems.

Rather than building only a RAG application, this project focuses on controlled experiments that separate:

- retrieval failures,
- evidence-sufficiency failures,
- and generation failures.

The broader goal is to study when retrieved evidence actually leads to more reliable answers and when additional context introduces noise.

---

## Research Question

**How do retrieval quality and evidence sufficiency influence the reliability of answers generated by RAG systems?**

---

## Current Research

### Experiment 01 — Effect of Retrieval Depth on RAG Reliability

The first experiment varies retrieval depth:

```text
k = 0   No retrieval
k = 1
k = 3
k = 5
k = 10
```

The experiment investigates whether retrieving more documents improves:

- supporting-document recall,
- complete evidence retrieval,
- answer Exact Match,
- token-level F1.

## Experiment 01A — Dense Retrieval Baseline

### Results

| k | Supporting Document Recall@K | Complete Evidence Rate@K |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 0.430 | 0.000 |
| 3 | 0.715 | 0.450 |
| 5 | 0.825 | 0.650 |
| 10 | 1.000 | 1.000 |

### Initial Observation

Increasing retrieval depth substantially improved evidence coverage.

At `k=1`, the retriever recovered only part of the evidence required for many multi-hop questions.

At `k=3` and `k=5`, both supporting-document recall and complete-evidence retrieval improved substantially.

At `k=10`, all candidate documents in the HotpotQA distractor context are retrieved, causing both retrieval metrics to reach 1.000.

This should **not** be interpreted as evidence that `k=10` is necessarily the optimal RAG configuration because it also introduces all distractor documents into the generation context.

## Experiment 01B — Retrieval Depth vs Answer Quality

Experiment 01B investigates whether improvements in retrieval depth
and evidence coverage translate into improvements in answer generation.

The experiment evaluates:

```text
100 questions × 5 retrieval conditions = 500 generations
```

### Results

| k | Exact Match | Token F1 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.240 | 0.326 |
| 1 | 0.200 | 0.276 |
| 3 | 0.210 | 0.309 |
| 5 | 0.330 | 0.452 |
| 10 | 0.390 | 0.559 |

### Combined Retrieval and Generation Results

| k | Supporting Recall | Complete Evidence | Exact Match | Token F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | — | — | 0.240 | 0.326 |
| 1 | 0.430 | 0.000 | 0.200 | 0.276 |
| 3 | 0.715 | 0.450 | 0.210 | 0.309 |
| 5 | 0.825 | 0.650 | 0.330 | 0.452 |
| 10 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.390 | 0.559 |

### Initial Observation

Retrieval quality improved consistently as retrieval depth increased.

Generation quality, however, did not improve monotonically at low
retrieval depths.

The no-retrieval baseline achieved an Exact Match score of 0.240 and
Token F1 of 0.326.

Providing only the top-1 retrieved document reduced performance to
0.200 Exact Match and 0.276 F1.

At k=3, retrieval coverage improved substantially, but generation
performance remained slightly below the no-retrieval baseline.

Generation performance improved more clearly once retrieval depth
reached k=5 and k=10.

The results suggest that simply supplying retrieved context is not
necessarily sufficient. Incomplete evidence may provide less useful
context than either more complete retrieval or, in some cases, no
retrieval at all.

Further analysis is required to determine whether these effects are
caused by evidence completeness, retrieval noise, generator behavior,
or the model's use of parametric knowledge.

## Evaluation Metrics

### Supporting Document Recall@K

Measures the proportion of gold supporting documents present among the top-k retrieved documents.

### Complete Evidence Rate@K

Measures whether all gold supporting documents required for a question are present in the retrieved context.

### Exact Match

Measures whether the normalized generated answer exactly matches the gold answer.

### Token-Level F1

Measures token overlap between the generated answer and the gold answer.

## Failure Analysis

The project will distinguish between several types of failure:

```text
1. Retrieval failure + generation failure
2. Retrieval success + generation success
3. Retrieval success + generation failure
4. Retrieval failure + generation success
5. Partial evidence retrieval
6. Retrieval noise
7. Unsupported answer generation
8. Answering despite insufficient evidence
```

This allows retrieval-stage and generation-stage errors to be analyzed separately.
